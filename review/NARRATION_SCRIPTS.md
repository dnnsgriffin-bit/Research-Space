# Evidence Studio: narration scripts

Two scripts, one for each video. Both are written for Dennis Griffin's own voice: first person, plain, unhurried.

Timecodes match the current videos. If you'd rather record at your own pace, record first and I'll re-time the video to your voice. That usually sounds more natural than squeezing words into fixed slots.

**Recording tips**

- Aim for about 140–150 words per minute. Each script is written to fit with room to breathe.
- Leave half a second of silence at the start and end.
- Record in a quiet, soft-furnished room. A phone held 15–20 cm from your mouth is fine.
- Save as WAV or high-quality M4A. Send one take per script; I'll handle trims and syncing.
- Smile slightly on the first and last lines. It changes the tone more than you'd expect.

## Script 1: The 45-second product demo

**Video:** `public/demo/evidence-check-demo.mp4` (about 45 seconds; the real tool, with on-screen captions)
**Audience:** L&D practitioners trying the tool for the first time
**Tone:** clear, practical, a guide sitting beside them
**Length:** about 115 words

| Time | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | The headline; the chain lights up | Training was completed. But did work change? Evidence Studio shows what your training data can honestly support. |
| 0:05–0:11 | Clicking common starting points | Start with what your report already shows: completion, a satisfaction survey, and a business number that went up. |
| 0:11–0:16 | Business outcome lit, the links between dark | The chain lights up only where something was observed. The result is lit, but everything in between is dark. |
| 0:16–0:21 | Inspector opens on "Opportunity to apply" | Open any gap to see what it can’t prove, and the cheapest evidence that would close it. |
| 0:21–0:28 | Ticking a skill check and on-the-job behavior | Then add what you really measured, like a skill check or an observation on the job. |
| 0:28–0:35 | Clicking “No” on manager support; a barrier appears | Check the workplace too. If managers don’t support the new behavior, more training won’t fix it. |
| 0:35–0:41 | The brief; “Copy brief” | You leave with a one-page brief: what you can say, what you can’t say yet, and a clear next decision. |
| 0:41–0:45 | End card | It’s free while it’s a prototype. Try it on your own program. |

**Plain text to read:**

> Training was completed. But did work change? Evidence Studio shows what your training data can honestly support.
>

>

### Wording guardrails (keep these if you ad-lib)

- Say “shows what your data can support,” never “proves impact” or “measures ROI.”
- Say “prototype” or “free while it’s a prototype,” never “launching” or “available now for teams.”
- Keep “research-based rules” rather than “AI” or “predicts.” The tool does not predict outcomes.
