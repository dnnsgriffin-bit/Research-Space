# Evidence Studio: award-level evaluation rubric

A weighted, evidence-anchored rubric for judging the Evidence Studio landing page (`/`) and the Evidence Check prototype (`/check`). It's written so that different reviewers, human or AI, produce comparable scores.

It combines three lenses:

1. **Award-jury craft.** Modeled on the categories design juries such as Awwwards use: design, usability, creativity and content.
2. **Evidence-based UX.** Nielsen’s usability heuristics, WCAG 2.2 AA, Mayer’s multimedia-learning principles (signaling, segmenting, coherence), and cognitive-load guidance for adult learners.
3. **Domain fitness.** Whether the product is accurate and useful for corporate learning and development, and credible to executives.

---

## How to score

1. Score each of the **9 categories from 1 to 5** using the anchors below. Half points are allowed.
2. Multiply by the weight: `points = weight × (score ÷ 5)`. The weights sum to **100**.
3. Check the **5 gates**. Any failed gate **caps the total at 69**, however strong everything else is.
4. For every score, cite **evidence**: a screen, an image file name or a quote. Mark each finding **Observed** (you saw it) or **Inferred** (you are reasoning from indirect evidence). If you couldn’t run the interactive pages, say so and lower your confidence.
5. Be strict. Award juries reject the large majority of entries. A 5 means best-in-class against the world’s leading product sites, not “good for a prototype.”

### Score bands

| Total | Band | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 90–100 | **Award contender** | Would compete for a site-of-the-day-level award and set a bar for the domain |
| 80–89 | **World class** | Polished, distinctive and trustworthy; minor refinements only |
| 70–79 | **Strong** | Professional and credible; a few issues hold it back |
| 60–69 | **Competent** | Works, but generic or inconsistent in places |
| Below 60 | **Needs work** | Clarity, trust or usability problems block success |

---

## Gates (pass or fail)

| # | Gate | Fails if |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | **No overclaiming** | Any copy or visual implies the tool proves causation, predicts impact or calculates ROI |
| G2 | **Examples are labeled** | Example or fictional data could be mistaken for real results |
| G3 | **Keyboard access** | Primary flows (navigation, hero tabs, the Evidence Check, FAQ, video) can’t be completed by keyboard, or focus isn’t visible |
| G4 | **Readable contrast** | Body text falls below 4.5:1 contrast, or essential text is unreadable on a phone |
| G5 | **Honest status** | The page implies the product is complete or for sale when it is a prototype |

## Categories, weights and anchors

### 1. Visual design and craft · weight 20
*Typography, color, composition, spacing, imagery, consistency, polish.*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Template-like or inconsistent; weak hierarchy; visual bugs |
| 3 | Clean and consistent; competent but forgettable; a few rough edges |
| 5 | A distinctive, coherent visual identity; masterful type scale and rhythm; color carries meaning; no visible flaws at any size |

Look for: type pairing and scale; restraint in color; whitespace rhythm across sections; alignment and baselines; image quality; consistency between the landing page and the tool.

### 2. Interaction and motion · weight 10
*Micro-interactions, transitions, scroll storytelling, feedback.*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Static, or motion that distracts or confuses |
| 3 | Smooth basic hover and press states; some decorative motion |
| 5 | Every animation explains a change of state; one memorable signature moment; scroll storytelling teaches the product; reduced-motion users get an equivalent experience |

Look for: the live hero chain; the guided scroll demo (cursor, clicks, typing); button feedback; the video poster; reduced-motion behavior.

### 3. Usability and information architecture · weight 15
*Nielsen’s heuristics, findability, flow and cognitive load.*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Visitors can’t tell what to do; navigation is confusing |
| 3 | The main path is clear; some sections are long or redundant |
| 5 | Within 5 seconds a first-time visitor knows what it is, who it’s for and the next action. The order of sections matches how buyers decide. There is no dead weight. |

Look for: the 5-second test; one clear primary action; menu length; section order; how easily someone can get from the landing page to the working tool and back.

### 4. Creativity and distinctiveness · weight 10
*Originality of concept and execution within its category.*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Indistinguishable from typical SaaS pages |
| 3 | Some fresh ideas within a familiar pattern |
| 5 | A concept only this product could have, such as light as evidence and the chain as identity, executed with conviction and memorable after one visit |

### 5. Content, copy and message clarity · weight 10
*Headline strength, plain language, scannability, persuasion without hype.*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Vague, jargon-heavy or hyped |
| 3 | Clear but generic; some long passages |
| 5 | Sharp, memorable headlines; plain language a busy executive can scan; every claim earned; calls to action say exactly what happens |

### 6. Evidence integrity and learning-science fidelity · weight 15
*Accuracy for corporate L&D; alignment with research on training evaluation and transfer.*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Misrepresents evaluation; treats completion or satisfaction as proof of impact |
| 3 | Directionally correct; some simplifications go unexplained |
| 5 | Measured, assumed and unknown are always distinguished; work-environment factors are integrated; research is cited and linked; limits are stated wherever a reader might overreach; Kirkpatrick and LTEM users will feel respected |

### 7. Trust, credibility and enterprise readiness · weight 8
*Would a VP of Talent or a CFO trust it and share it?*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Feels like a side project; no credible creator or rationale |
| 3 | Credible creator and rationale; light on proof |
| 5 | Creator expertise is clear early; privacy is explicit; pricing is honest; the output (the brief) looks board-ready; it’s easy to share with leadership |

Note: the absence of testimonials is expected at prototype stage. Score what is present, and don’t reward fabricated social proof.

### 8. Accessibility and inclusive design · weight 7
*WCAG 2.2 AA and inclusive practice.*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Major barriers for keyboard, screen-reader or low-vision users |
| 3 | Basics in place; some contrast or labeling gaps |
| 5 | Meets AA throughout: semantic structure, visible focus, accessible names, captions and text alternatives, reduced-motion equivalents, and color never the only signal |

### 9. Responsive quality and performance · weight 5
*Phones, tablets, large screens; load weight; smoothness.*

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Broken or cramped on phones; slow or janky |
| 3 | Works on phones, with some compromises |
| 5 | Feels designed for every size, with readable demos on a phone, no horizontal scroll, a reasonable page weight and smooth motion |

## Persona checks (score each 1–5)

| Persona | Question to answer | Score |
|---|---|---|
| L&D practitioner | “Could I use this on a real program today and send the brief to my stakeholder?” | |
| L&D or talent leader | “Would this help me decide whether to scale, fix or stop a program?” | |
| Executive or CFO | “In 30 seconds, do I understand what this tells me and trust it?” | |
| Learning scientist or evaluator | “Is it accurate, and does it respect existing frameworks?” | |
| Design-award juror | “Is it distinctive, crafted and memorable enough to win?” | |
| Accessibility auditor | “Can everyone use it?” | |
